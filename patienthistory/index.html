Case study three — Product design

Design in three acts: the patient history feature

APODIENSTE, 2024 — PharmaBits

Figma / FigJam / Notion / Maze

Product designer, end-to-end contributor — partnered with the product design lead across discovery, information architecture, interaction design, research and delivery.

Context

apodienste helps pharmacies deliver these services at scale. Early on, all communication lived in a single free-text comment box. Operators had to scroll, copy and retype notes to turn them into next steps, and context was frequently lost between calls.

Constraints

"How might we make teammate communication actionable, searchable and reliably documented across multi-call workflows, without increasing cognitive load or documentation overhead?"

Problem statement
  1. Live production product — changes had to ship incrementally.
  2. Auditability and legal retention requirements, covering agreements and receipts.
  3. Roles with different "work speeds" — operators versus pharmacists and analysts.

Process — three acts

"We have to use the comment section to track and record what has been done before for the patient, but it quickly becomes cluttered and hard to follow the important information."

Lorina, call-centre operator
  1. Act 1 — actionable mentions. Transform unstructured comments into traceable work.
  2. Act 2 — the action panel. Keep context visible while acting.
  3. Act 3 — guided action modals. Driven by an action taxonomy, so work can be completed without returning to comments.

Act one — actionable mentions

Goal: transform unstructured comments into traceable work.

Discovery

Session replays and interviews showed operators using the comment box both to talk to teammates and to carry out actions. Notes were short and fragmented, and important items got buried.

Define

Users needed a place to log events, actions and medical information — then remove resolved items from view. The key need: a lightweight way to turn something someone said into something the system could act on.

Develop

Introduced @mentions that bind to actions — people, cases, services — and a resolve mechanism to clear completed items from the stream.

Deliver & impact

Shipped mentions and resolving for comments, giving call-centre operators a comprehensive workflow and producing a notable drop in missed cases and lost information.

Act two — the action panel

Goal: eliminate the back-and-forth between comments and action modals.

Discovery

Microsoft Clarity recordings showed operators repeatedly bouncing between an action modal and the comment section to check "what happened last."

Define

The pain point was time cost, cognitive load and distraction from the main flow. The design challenge was to keep context visible while acting.

Develop

Co-created with development and product through Crazy-8s, the team added an action panel that surfaces the latest case and patient actions alongside the current task, and lets operators trigger common actions without leaving context.

Validate and iterate

Categorisation survey 86%

accuracy for the AI-assisted grouping behind the new action taxonomy.

Pre-launch usability tests found users enthusiastic, and they helped distinguish "high-frequency" from "rare or risky" actions. A thematic analysis of comments — interviews, clustering, AI-assisted categorisation — produced an action taxonomy; a survey on the categorisation showed roughly 86% accuracy for the AI-assisted grouping.

Deliver

Shipped the action panel and aligned the interface around the new action taxonomy.

Act three — guided action modals

Goal: guide operators through the exact actions they take before and after calls, without returning to comments.

Discovery

After act two, bouncing between screens reduced, but many users still scrolled to comments to leave or update notes during and after calls.

Define

The team mapped the precise actions operators take before and after call notes. These matched the themes from the thematic analysis, which meant the team could design for the actions directly, rather than for comments.

Develop

Introduced guided action modals that keep users in context: clear primary actions (changing case status, scheduling an event, adding a service), contextual confirmations and next-step prompts, and system notifications for downstream stakeholders. The original comment use-cases were migrated into main actions, so operators no longer needed comments for operational steps.

Deliver & impact

Usability tests showed operators realised they no longer needed to navigate to the comment section to complete work. Business-critical actions were prioritised first, with the rest sequenced into the delivery queue.

Takeaways

  1. Action-centric flows. Design operations first and let context be captured implicitly, to reduce errors and wrap-up time.
  2. Evidence before pixels. Treat comments and behaviours as research signals — synthesise, qualitatively and with light quantitative analysis, before solutioning.
  3. Measure and iterate. Instrument key events, ship incrementally, and refine with accessibility and resilience guardrails.

Outcome

30%decrease in the number of clicks
50%decrease in task completion time
  1. Reduced unnecessary navigation.
  2. Streamlined workflows.
  3. Improved focus on tasks — operators reported less time spent context-switching between comments and action modals.
