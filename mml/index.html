Case study five — Archive UX

Redesigning a historical archive for the digital age

MARX MEMORIAL LIBRARY, 2024 — Historical archive

Figma / FigJam

UX designer, in a mixed team of two graphic designers and three UX designers — led core UX work on information architecture, user flows and the website redesign.

Context & my role

The Marx Memorial Library (MML) asked our mixed team — two graphic designers, three UX designers — to modernise its brand and digital experience. I led core UX work on information architecture, user flows and the website redesign.

Problem framing

On-site observations showed rich content but a hard-to-navigate experience, both physically and digitally. Visual inconsistencies, unclear wayfinding and scattered tools made it difficult for visitors and remote researchers to engage.

"How might we transform the Marx Memorial Library's static and hard-to-navigate website into an engaging, research-focused platform that makes discovery intuitive, supports deep research workflows, and broadens access beyond the physical library?"

Problem statement

Insight & strategy

Insight. Existing designs leaned "museum-like" — visually rich, but not optimised for deep, searchable collections.

Strategy. Rebuild information architecture first (sitemap, taxonomy), then layer a modular UI and research-centric features such as personalisation and contribution tools.

Process

  1. Research & discovery. Visited the library, analysed the existing website and branding, and benchmarked against similar archives to understand user needs, pain points and opportunities.
  2. Define & synthesise. Created personas and clarified core user goals, shaping the project vision and guiding design decisions.
  3. Ideation and sitemapping. Reorganised content into a clear, intuitive sitemap and navigation structure to improve findability and support research workflows.
  4. Wireframing & user flows. Sketched low-fidelity wireframes and mapped user journeys to test layout ideas and validate task flows early.
  5. UI design & branding. Developed a modern visual language aligned with the new brand identity, designed modular components, and built consistent layouts.
  6. Prototype & iterate. Created interactive prototypes, refined based on feedback, and improved clarity, hierarchy and visual coherence across pages.

With a clear focus on user needs, I defined three core objectives to guide our design decisions: enhancing the research experience; optimising website structure and navigation ease; and accessibility and personalisation.

Research & discovery

Key findings

  1. Users struggled to find and access materials due to poor navigation and unclear structure.
  2. The website felt outdated and failed to reflect the richness of the archive.
  3. Researchers needed tools for deeper exploration, saving and contributing content.
  4. Remote users lacked meaningful ways to engage with the library's resources.

Define and synthesise

Ideation and sitemapping

The branding team focused on visual identity — logos, typography, colour schemes and visual templates — while the UX team, where I contributed most, focused on restructuring the website, proposing new user flows, and improving access to resources.

The typeface and colour palette were chosen collaboratively. We selected Poppins to align with the preferences of our target audience, and opted for a set of muted colours to create a modern yet approachable visual tone.

Wireframing and user flows

Low-fidelity wireframes and user journey maps tested layout ideas and validated task flows early, before any visual design work began.

UI design concept

We established a cohesive visual identity using clean typography, a muted yet contemporary colour palette, and modular components that could scale across the site. The homepage became the foundation for our design language, introducing clear calls-to-action, improved search functionality and intuitive navigation paths. This approach modernised the look and feel of the Marx Memorial Library and transformed the website into a dynamic, user-friendly platform that invites exploration, learning and community engagement.

Special pages, features and functionalities

The profile page lets users save favourites, continue past research, and discover suggested resources based on their interests — turning the archive into a more personal and engaging research tool.

A "Scan & Add" feature lets users contribute directly to the digital archive by scanning books or documents with their mobile devices — a scalable solution that supports the library's digitisation efforts and makes visitors active participants in growing the collection.

Style & component library

A style and component library kept the design consistent, scalable and easy to hand over to developers — defining a cohesive colour system, typography hierarchy and reusable components, ensuring visual harmony across pages and speeding up future iterations.

Solution proposal & delivery

Next time, I would…

  1. Push for stronger collaboration on consistency. Work more closely with the graphic design team from the beginning, to ensure brand identity and visual language stay fully consistent across the entire experience.
  2. Push beyond boundaries. Explore more innovative interaction patterns and bolder design directions, to challenge conventional structures and deliver a more distinctive experience.
  3. Involve users and test sooner. Bring users into the process earlier and test iteratively, to validate ideas and refine decisions based on real feedback.
