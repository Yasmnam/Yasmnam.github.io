Case study four — Archive UX

Redesigning a historical archive for the digital age

MARX MEMORIAL LIBRARY, 2024 — London

Figma / FigJam

UX designer on a mixed team of two graphic designers and three UX designers — led core UX work on information architecture, user flows and the website redesign.

Context & my role

The Marx Memorial Library asked the team to modernise its brand and digital experience. On-site observations showed rich content but a hard-to-navigate experience, both physically and digitally — visual inconsistencies, unclear wayfinding and scattered tools made it difficult for visitors and remote researchers to engage.

"How might we transform the Marx Memorial Library's static and hard-to-navigate website into an engaging, research-focused platform that makes discovery intuitive, supports deep research workflows, and broadens access beyond the physical library?"

Problem statement

Insight & strategy

Insight — the existing design leaned "museum-like": visually rich, but not optimised for deep, searchable collections.

Strategy — rebuild the information architecture first — sitemap, taxonomy — then layer a modular interface and research-centric features such as personalisation and contribution tools on top.

Process

  1. Research & discovery. Visited the library, analysed the existing website and branding, and benchmarked against similar archives to understand user needs, pain points and opportunities.
  2. Define & synthesise. Created personas and clarified core user goals, shaping the project vision and guiding design decisions.
  3. Ideation and sitemapping. Reorganised content into a clear, intuitive sitemap and navigation structure to improve findability and support research workflows.
  4. Wireframing & user flows. Sketched low-fidelity wireframes and mapped user journeys to test layout ideas and validate task flows early.
  5. UI design & branding. Developed a modern visual language aligned with the new brand identity, designed modular components, and built consistent layouts.
  6. Prototype & iterate. Created interactive prototypes, refined based on feedback, and improved clarity, hierarchy and visual coherence across pages.

Three core objectives guided every design decision: enhancing the research experience, optimising website structure and navigation ease, and accessibility and personalisation.

Research & discovery

  1. Users struggled to find and access materials due to poor navigation and unclear structure.
  2. The website felt outdated and failed to reflect the richness of the archive.
  3. Researchers needed tools for deeper exploration, saving and contributing content.
  4. Remote users lacked meaningful ways to engage with the library's resources.

Wireframing & user flows

Low-fidelity wireframes for the homepage, collections, archives, about and support pages set out layout and hierarchy before any visual design began, so structural decisions could be tested independently of styling.

UI design & branding

The branding team focused on visual identity — logo, typography, colour and templates — while the UX team, where this contribution sat, restructured the website, proposed new user flows and improved access to resources. Poppins was chosen as the typeface to align with the target audience's preferences, paired with a set of muted colours for a modern, approachable tone.

A cohesive visual identity — clean typography, a muted yet contemporary palette, and modular components that could scale across the site — turned the archive into a more dynamic, user-friendly platform inviting exploration, learning and community engagement.

Special pages & features

A profile page lets users save favourites, continue past research, and discover suggested resources based on their interests — turning the archive into a more personal, engaging research tool. A "Scan & Add" feature lets visitors contribute directly to the digital archive by scanning books or documents on their mobile device, supporting the library's digitisation efforts while making visitors active participants in growing the collection.

Solution & delivery

The final brand and print collateral carried the new identity across formats — a style and component library kept the digital design consistent, scalable, and easy to hand over to developers.

Next time, I would…

  1. Push for stronger collaboration on consistency. Work more closely with the graphic design team from the beginning, so brand identity and visual language stay fully consistent across the entire experience.
  2. Push beyond the obvious boundaries. Explore more innovative interaction patterns and bolder design directions to challenge conventional structures and deliver a more distinctive experience.
  3. Bring in testing and users sooner. Test iteratively from earlier in the process to validate ideas and refine decisions based on real feedback.
