Case study four — Sensory data

Turning ambient data into emotional predictability

SENSECAST, 2025 — Self-directed

TouchDesigner / Figma / Figma Make / FigJam

Self-directed project — research, visual language, TouchDesigner prototyping, and UI design across mobile, public screen and wearable concepts.

Problem

Public environments are unpredictable — noise, light and crowd levels change constantly. For people with sensory sensitivities or anxiety, that unpredictability can be overwhelming and lead to avoidance.

Existing solutions, such as occupancy apps and weather forecasts, address the practical layer but fail to address the emotional layer of spatial experience.

Process

"How might we visualise sensory conditions in a way that reduces emotional discomfort and builds trust before people enter a space?"

Problem statement
  1. Research & discovery. Investigated how unpredictable public spaces affect people with sensory sensitivities, through surveys, interviews and psychological studies.
  2. Concept development. Developed personas and user journeys, and framed the core challenge: translating ambient data into emotional previews.
  3. Ideation & visual language. Explored how shape, colour and motion influence emotion, and defined a calming, human-centred visual style.
  4. Design & prototyping. Created wireframes and interactive prototypes for mobile, public screens and wearables, focused on intuitive user flows.
  5. Technical exploration. Built audio-reactive visualisations in TouchDesigner and used AI tools to accelerate iteration and mock-up creation.
  6. Testing & delivery. Delivered a cohesive brand identity, functional prototypes, and a real-time visualisation concept demonstrating the full user experience.

Research — grounding in human needs

Surveys and interviews explored how people perceive and navigate public spaces. Psychological studies investigated how shapes, colours and sensory inputs affect emotional response. Benchmarking against weather apps and ambient interfaces showed how soft, uncertain data can still be presented clearly.

A calm and emotionally supportive interface emerges from the thoughtful combination of sensory science and visual design. Rounded shapes, organic forms and soft colour transitions turn complex sensory information into an accessible, reassuring experience — a principle that became central to SenseCast's visual language.

Key insight

Concept development — from idea to vision

Role-based personas and user journeys mapped needs and behaviours. Key sensory inputs — sound, light, crowd density, colour and social cues — were identified, and an impact–feasibility matrix prioritised the most meaningful data. The concept settled on transforming ambient data into emotional previews that support comfort and predictability.

Design concept: a predictive "emotional weather forecast" for spaces, visualised through soft, fluid graphics that represent sensory conditions at a glance.

Design & prototyping — bringing SenseCast to life

Sensory visual explorations

TouchDesigner translated sensory data into dynamic visuals — audio-reactive elements, fluid forms and particle systems standing in for sound, light and crowd density. The learning curve with the tool expanded technical range and deepened an understanding of how data can drive emotional, atmospheric design.

Brand identity design

The logo and visual language use fluid forms and glassmorphism effects to capture the idea of "casting" sensory data, while maintaining a calm and approachable character.

Wireframing and structure: wireframes and user flows defined the core interactions and navigation of the product.

Vibecoding & creative process

Vibecoding — working conversationally with an AI coding assistant — sped up iteration and acted as a creative partner, generating new visual directions and strengthening the ability to combine computational logic with emotional storytelling.

UI design

High-fidelity interfaces for mobile, public screens and a wearable extension used soft gradients, organic shapes and a calming colour palette, translating complex sensory data into clear, emotionally supportive experiences.

Testing & future steps

The concept was tested beyond the screen too: a public-screen projection let people experience the ambient forecast in a shared space, and peer review sessions gathered reactions to colour and texture treatments across a set of simulated destinations, testing which visual moods read as calm, safe or overwhelming, and to whom.

Next time, I would…

  1. Place more emphasis on testing. Deeper, more frequent user testing throughout the process would have given stronger insight into how people emotionally respond to the visuals and interactions.
  2. Address accessibility in the visual design. The glassmorphism style achieved a calm, soothing aesthetic, but its accessibility limitations needed more thorough attention — particularly given a target audience that is sensitive and requires clear, inclusive design.
  3. Improve the showcase experience. The projection concept communicated the idea well, but the presentation and setup could have been refined further for a more impactful showcase.
